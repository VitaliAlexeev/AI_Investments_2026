Stress Lab

Machine learning learns from the months it has seen. A stress test asks about the months it has not. Three experiments from Lecture 10 of AI-powered Investment and Risk Management, on real US data.

Hatching marks where the data run out.

Four models learn how US bank stocks move with the market from a training window of monthly data. Drag the market shock into the hatched region, past the worst month they were trained on, and compare what each model predicts.

Months since 1970 are sorted by the market return. Each map shows the correlations between 49 US industries, in calm months (the middle half) and in the worst months. Darker means the industries move together, so diversification does less.

Choose a quarterly loss for a stock and Treasury portfolio and the history your model learns from. The orange point is the most plausible quarter that delivers the loss. The ellipse joins quarters exactly as plausible; the hatched region is every quarter that breaches.

History the model learns from

Data: Kenneth R. French Data Library (49 industry portfolios and the market factor, CRSP June 2026 vintage) and Goyal–Welch predictor data to December 2025, as mirrored in the AIRM teaching repository. The Treasury series compounds to −12.5% in 2022, closer to a 10-year than a 20-year bond.

Experiment 1 uses models fitted in Python with scikit-learn (random forest: 500 trees, at least 5 months per leaf; gradient boosting: scikit-learn's histogram version) and embedded here. Experiments 2 and 3 are computed live in your browser.
